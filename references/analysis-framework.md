# Analysis Framework: the report dimensions in full

For each dimension: ask the question, record a transferable rule rather than a caption of the source, and grade every value observed, inferred or unknown as defined in SKILL.md. Dimensions 4, 6 and 9 contain the values that can only ever be inferred from a finished image.

## 1. Medium and realism

Photo, realistic or semi-realistic 3D, illustration of a named visible kind, product render, UI or graphic design, mixed medium. What gives it away: skin pores and sensor noise, brush or ink behavior, render smoothness and perfect reflections, vector flatness. Record the medium word plus a realism level from 1 to 5.

## 2. Composition geometry

Frame orientation and ratio, subject position (left, right, center, a named third) and share of the frame, the foreground midground background split with what sits in each plane, symmetry or asymmetry, leading lines and frames within frames, the eye path in order first second third, and where the negative space is and how much of the frame it takes.

## 3. Shot scale and camera position

Close-up, medium, full body, wide or aerial. Cropping tight or airy. Camera height and angle: eye level, high, low, top-down, dutch. Subject-to-environment ratio.

## 4. Optics, lens and depth of field

Judge by effect first: wide expansion and stretched edges, natural perspective, or telephoto compression and flattened layers. Focus plane position, depth of field shallow or deep and how it falls off, bokeh shape and quality, foreground blur. Only then give the inferred reading: an approximate focal length with a tilde, an approximate aperture, always labeled inferred and always next to the effect that grounds it. If the effects conflict, write unknown and say which effects conflict.

## 5. Light source

The motivated in-world source a viewer can point to: window, direct flash, neon sign, overcast sky, practical lamp, noon sun. Its direction in clock terms or plain words, its height, and any secondary sources, bounce or practicals visible in frame.

## 6. Light quality, temperature and ratio

Hard or soft and the shadow edge that proves it, feathered, crisp or crunchy. Contrast between lit and shadow side as a range, for example about 2:1 to 4:1, labeled inferred. Color temperature as an inferred Kelvin range with the visible cast that grounds it. Highlight behavior: clipped hotspots, gentle rolloff, halation bloom. Catchlight shape when eyes are visible.

## 7. Exposure and dynamic range

High key, low key or mid. Black point lifted and milky or crushed and deep. Whether highlights clip or roll off, whether shadow detail survives, and the overall tonal curve in words.

## 8. Palette

Five colors as HEX with plain names, labeled eyeballed approximation or tool-measured. Then the split: dominant, supporting and accent shares, roughly 60 30 10 when it fits, where saturation concentrates (one hero accent, skin only, or global), and the hue relationships: complementary, analogous, split or monochrome, warm against cool and where each lives.

## 9. Grade and finish

The grading behavior in words: faded matte blacks, teal shadows against warm skin, high-saturation commercial pop, bleach-bypass desaturation. A film stock or digital finish label when the grain and color response support one, always inferred. Grain size and character, halation, chromatic aberration, sharpening, compression or scan traces, each present or explicitly absent.

## 10. Materials and texture

At least three tactile surfaces, each as material plus light response: skin with pores or sheen, fabric weave named by type, metal brushed or polished, glass, ceramic, wood grain, paper, food surfaces. Note where texture concentrates and where the image goes smooth.

## 11. Atmosphere and background logic

Physical air: haze, dust, steam, rain sheen, smoke, or a clean void. Background kind: storytelling environment, seamless void, blurred crowd, graphic flat, layered landscape. Its depth, detail level and whether it narrates, isolates the subject, or decorates.

## 12. Era and styling language

Period and cultural codes carried by wardrobe, hair, props, product design, typography or architecture. Name the code by its visible features, not by a borrowed famous name alone.

## 13. Mood and psychology

The viewer's feeling and its concrete causes: contrast, color isolation, lens distance, gaze direction, density. Viewing distance as confrontational, intimate, observational or voyeuristic. A mood word without its causes fails this dimension.

## 14. Density and instantaneity

Information density from 1 to 5 and where it concentrates. Negative space amount and location. The moment state: posed and held, candid drift, frozen mid-action, suspended. The shutter feel that supports it, flash-frozen or motion-blurred.

## Short form

When time or image simplicity does not justify the full pass, cover at minimum medium, composition, light source and quality, optics by effect, palette, texture and mood, then the visual anchors. Never ship a report without palette, light and lens-effect sections, with grades.
