# Light and Color Vocabulary

Controlled terms for reports, so two images analyzed on different days stay comparable. These words fill the report, the image supplies the evidence, and the grade from SKILL.md states the certainty. Kelvin values, ratios and film stocks are always graded inferred when read from a finished image.

## Color temperature ranges

- 2700 to 3200K: warm tungsten, practical lamps, domestic night, amber skin.
- 3400 to 3800K: warm morning sun through a window, golden slats, terracotta bounce.
- 4200 to 4500K: mixed interior, warm fluorescent, office realism.
- 5000 to 5600K: daylight, overcast, direct flash, neutral editorial.
- 6000 to 7000K: cool shade, blue hour, melancholy or tech-clean.

## Light quality and contrast ratio

- Soft wrap-around diffused, about 1.5:1 to 2:1: overcast, beauty studio, seamless e-commerce.
- Naturalistic soft directional, about 2.5:1: window light through a drape or scrim.
- Moody cinematic, about 3:1 to 4:1: side key falling off into a shadow floor.
- Hard direct, about 4:1 to 8:1: on-camera flash, sharp nose shadow, wall falloff hotspot.
- Harsh noon sun, 8:1 and above: crunchy nose and chin shadows, usually a flaw to name rather than a look to copy.

Shadow edge words: feathered, crisp, crunchy, lifted and faded, crushed and deep. Catchlight words: round flash catchlight, window rectangle catchlight, absent.

## Film stocks and digital finishes

Pick at most one, and only when grain and color response support it, graded inferred.

- Kodak Portra 400: warm creamy skin, fine grain, lifestyle and editorial.
- Kodak Vision3 500T: tungsten-balanced cinema, deep blacks, amber halation, night interiors.
- Kodak Vision3 250D: daylight crisp, true primaries, day exteriors.
- Kodak Tri-X 400: punchy high-grain monochrome-leaning documentary.
- Fuji Eterna 500: desaturated pastel shadows, arthouse softness.
- Direct-flash digital: flat, sharp, slight softness, phone or compact camera feel.
- Old smartphone or Y2K digicam: compressed highlights, noisy shadows, built-in flash blowout.
- Halftone or risograph print: visible dots, ink texture, paper seams, handmade print character.

## Grade names

Kodak 2383 print curve, matte faded blacks, teal shadows against amber skin split tone, bleach-bypass desaturation, high-saturation commercial pop, warm sepia lifted shadows, cyan shadow undertone, clean neutral commercial clarity.

## Texture nouns

Skin: visible pores, peach fuzz, subsurface sheen, flash sheen on forehead. Fabric: coarse canvas weave, ribbed cable knit, denim twill, velour, sheer linen, crinkled nylon. Surface: glazed ceramic, brushed stainless, weathered timber, knurled chrome, matte plastic, glass transparency, bread crumb, terracotta clay. Pair every texture with its light response, glossy, matte, translucent or scattering.

## Grain, halation and post traces

Grain: ultra-fine 35mm, fine organic, punchy coarse, digital chroma noise. Halation: golden-red window bloom, amber lamp bloom, blue anamorphic streak, used sparingly for stills. Traces: dust motes, light-leak edge, chromatic aberration fringe, halftone dots, scan scratches, compression banding, sharpening halos, diffusion-filter veil over the whole frame.
